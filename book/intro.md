# Introduction: Why I Wrote This Book

In 2008, I bought a stock because it was all over the news. It tanked, and I was out $2,000—my first big investing lesson, though not my last mistake. Not even close.

My name is Chad, and I'm a 56-year-old guy living in Seattle, Washington. I'm not a Wall Street hotshot. I'm not a millionaire (yet). I'm not a social media influencer—I considered that path during the pandemic, but it's just not me. I'm not a financial advisor, so do your own research and consult one if you need professional guidance. If you're reading this from outside the US, your mileage may vary with some of the tax-advantaged accounts I'll discuss, but the core principles remain the same.

I'm just an investor who has made mistakes and learned the hard way. This book is for folks like me, and you, who want to build wealth the "right" way—and I put that in quotes because ultimately, it's your call how you define success. But if you want to retire with financial freedom, supported by cash flow from assets you've built over time, then we're climbing the same mountain.

## Why This Matters More Than You Think

I started investing in June of 2000, but I wasted the first 20 years. I'm now in my 50s, hoping and praying that I have enough time to build a real portfolio, a real nest egg, and a real revenue stream before I'm unable to work anymore. Twenty years. Gone. Thrown away on strategies that felt right at the time but led me nowhere.

Here's why I'm telling you this: I don't want you wasting your time on the wrong paths. And this isn't just about you and me. Lost years hurt more than just your retirement account—they hurt our shared future, a civilization that depends on people having strong financial foundations. I'm not kidding about this. I really do care, and making mistakes and wasting time hurts all of us. Our economy, our communities, our families—they all function better when more people achieve financial stability. When you waste time, you're not just postponing your own freedom; you're weakening the foundation we all stand on.

So don't waste your time. Learn what the real goal of investing is, and build a strategy that will help you get where you want to go.

## The Three Eras of My Investing Life

Let me give you the quick version of my journey, so you understand where I'm coming from and why I built Total Growth Investing (TGI) the way I did.

### 2000–2010: Dart-Throwing Days

I threw darts at stocks that I saw in the news, hoping they would go up. That's not investing—that's gambling with a business suit on. I bought AT&T because it felt safe with over a century of history behind it. I bought Hewlett-Packard because I owned one of their computers. Classic beginner mistakes: I was picking names I recognized, not companies I'd researched. Some got lucky and worked out, like Texas Instruments, which hooked me on investing in the first place. But most didn't. HPQ I sold after seven years, right before it soared—should have kept that one. AT&T I held until recently, watching it stagnate for years—should have sold that one. That first decade, I wasn't an investor or a trader. I was clueless, and my portfolio showed it.

### 2010–2019: High-Yield Trap

Then came my second wasted decade. The 2008 financial crisis hit everyone hard, and my portfolio value shrank along with everyone else's. But here's what I noticed: my dividends held steady even as prices collapsed. That observation sparked an obsession with high-yield stocks. I started chasing things like Oxford Lane Capital (OXLC) with its shiny 20% dividend yield, thinking I'd struck gold and could get rich quick by getting lucky with big dividend checks.

Wrong. Dead wrong.

Those shiny objects turned out to be black holes. They collapsed in the 2020 pandemic, and many of them don't even exist anymore. I learned the hard way that sky-high yields often hide crumbling foundations. My second decade was a high-yield hunt that burned me, teaching me that if something looks too good to be true, it probably is.

### 2019–Present: TGI Turnaround

In summer 2019, a friend pushed me to rethink everything. He challenged my entire approach, and instead of getting defensive, I took it seriously. I didn't start from scratch—I took my existing watchlist and reformatted it, scoring stocks by three key metrics: dividend growth, price growth, and low payout ratios (how much a company pays out versus what it earns). 

The math was brutal. Most of my holdings ranked at the very bottom. These were the stocks I'd spent years accumulating, and the numbers were telling me they were garbage.

In 2020, with my portfolio down to $20,248.60 after the market crash, I acted. I sold nearly everything. I kept Texas Instruments (TXN) and Comcast (CMCSA)—a very few others that scored high enough to survive. Letting go of the rest, especially stocks that had been battered by the crash, was tough. I kept thinking maybe they'd recover. But the numbers didn't lie, and I finally cut my losses and moved on.

With the freed-up cash, I bought top-ranked stocks like Home Depot for $219 and Visa. I was planting seeds for future growth, buying companies that my new ranking system told me were the best of the best.

The results? My $20,248.60 grew to $140,861.56 by February 2026—a 596% total return over six years. That growth was fueled by rising stock prices and growing dividends—Visa, for example, raises its dividend by roughly 10% every year. Switching to TGI meant my income took a severe hit at first—a 90% drop that stayed on a downward path for a year and a half. That was scary. I won't lie to you about that. I watched my dividend revenue crater, and I had to trust the system I'd built. But what kept me going was tracking my portfolio balance, and seeing that even though my income had collapsed, my total portfolio value wasn't crashing at all. It stayed steady as a rock through the global pandemic and into the recovery. Even the severe downturn in late 2022 didn't shake my portfolio's foundation.

By late 2022, I started seeing my revenue numbers pick back up again, growing every time one of my stocks raised their dividend. Total Growth Investing (TGI) became my plan, with my ranked watchlist as the map, turning dreams into plans, and plans into progress.

I wish I'd started sooner. Of course I do. But the calendar only turns in one direction. With this strategy, I'm climbing toward retirement in around 10 years with a revenue stream and freedom within sight. Your resources can spark the same journey. Let me show you how.

## What You'll Learn in This Book

This isn't a book about secrets or insider tips. It's not about timing the market perfectly or finding the next hot stock before everyone else. This is about building a system—a repeatable, data-driven approach that answers one simple question every time you have money to invest: "What do I buy next?"

Total Growth Investing is built on discipline, not luck. It's built on ranking stocks by measurable criteria, not on hunches or headlines. It's built for the long haul, not for quick wins that evaporate the moment market conditions shift.

You'll learn:

- **The Four Quadrants framework** that maps your entire financial picture: Income, Assets, Debts, and Expenses
- **How to build a ranked watchlist** that spots the best stocks for both dividend growth and price appreciation
- **The three core metrics** I use to rank hundreds of stocks and funds
- **How to diversify without overthinking it** (my simple 5% and 10% rules)
- **When to buy, when to hold, and when to sell** based on data, not emotions
- **How to transition from growth to income** as you approach retirement (my Healthy Income Investing strategy)
- **Real talk about mistakes**: high-yield traps, Bitcoin scams, and lessons learned the expensive way

This is a system you can replicate. My watchlists are public and open-source. I believe in transparency because I believe in helping people, not gatekeeping information. If what I've built works for you, use it. If you want to modify it for your own situation, even better. The important thing is that you have a plan, and you stick to it.

## How This Book Came to Be

This book started as lazy copy-paste. I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

I was answering questions in Facebook investing groups, and I realized I was typing the same advice over and over. So I started saving my responses in a document because it was easier to copy-paste than retype the same explanations again and again. That document grew. Questions kept coming, and my answers kept getting longer and more detailed, covering more and more topics. Eventually, I realized I could just share the whole document instead of hunting for specific sections to paste into comment threads.

In January 2025, I published the first version on Amazon Kindle. Then I kept refining it, editing for clarity and impact, because I wanted it to be genuinely helpful to as many people as possible. This is the result of that process: a book that's been tested in the real world, answering real questions from real investors who are trying to figure out how to build wealth without getting burned.

Let's get started.
